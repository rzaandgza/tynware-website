Markup vs Margin: Contractor Pricing Calculator

Understand markup versus profit margin, work through a labour and materials example, and calculate a selling price from your costs and target margin.

Published by Tynware · November 12, 2026

A 30% markup does not give you a 30% margin. Both compare profit with another number, but the denominator changes. That distinction matters when you price materials, labour and services for a customer quote.

Markup uses cost. Margin uses selling price.

  • Markup % = (selling price − cost) ÷ cost × 100
  • Margin % = (selling price − cost) ÷ selling price × 100

If a job costs 100 and you sell it for 130, the profit is 30. The markup is 30 ÷ 100 = 30%, but the margin is 30 ÷ 130 = 23.08%. Use the same currency for every amount.

A materials and labour example

Suppose materials cost 240 and labour costs 160. The entered cost is 400. To earn a 25% margin on those costs, calculate 400 ÷ 0.75 = 533.33. The difference is 133.33, equivalent to a 33.33% markup.

Adding only 25% to the cost would produce a price of 500 and a margin of 20%. The price can look reasonable while missing the margin you intended.

Which costs belong in the quote?

Start with the costs you expect to incur for the work: materials, paid labour, subcontracted services and job-specific expenses. Keep your own labour visible too. Decide how you account for overhead and allowances before you use the result as a customer price.

The calculator shows the margin on the costs you enter. It cannot know about omitted travel, administration, rework, overhead or income tax, so the result is not automatically your final net profit. Round the customer price deliberately and check the resulting margin again.

Keep internal costs separate from the customer PDF

Your working quote needs costs and margin so you can judge the job. The customer needs a clear description of the work, quantities, selling prices and relevant terms. Using one number for both purposes makes it harder to review whether the work is worthwhile.

Common pricing questions

Can margin reach 100%?

With positive costs, a 100% margin cannot be reached at a finite selling price. The calculator therefore accepts a target below 100%.

What happens when the cost is zero?

The price-from-cost formula returns zero. Percentage markup is undefined because it would divide by zero. A zero cost is not enough information to choose a useful selling price.

Does the calculation include VAT or sales tax?

No. Compare costs and selling prices on the same tax basis, then handle the applicable sales tax separately when preparing the final quote.
