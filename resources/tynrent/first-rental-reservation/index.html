Your first rental reservation in TynRent.

Work through a small event booking: add equipment, check availability, confirm quantities, export a quote and record the return.

Explore TynRentSee the worked example

Start with demonstration data.

Download TynRent from its official product page and follow the installation and platform guidance there. This walkthrough is a practice scenario for a new or test dataset. The item names and prices are fictional.

Use a few representative items before entering a full catalogue. If you already have business records in the application, create a current backup before experimenting. Do not replace or delete your working data to follow a tutorial.

1. Add the equipment you can actually rent.

Create Folding chair with quantity 30, and Folding table with quantity 6. For this example, use a unit price of EUR 5 for a chair and EUR 15 for a table. Check the price stored on the reservation rather than assuming an automatic daily-rate calculation.

2. Create one reservation.

Use a fictional customer and an event such as Sample garden reception. Choose 5 December 2026 at 09:00 through 6 December at 18:00, then add 20 chairs and 4 tables. Check the currency and each quantity before confirming.

3. Check the conflict protection.

With that booking confirmed, try a second reservation over the same period for 15 chairs and 3 tables. The original stock cannot cover both requests. Check the shortage message and keep the second reservation in Draft while you change the quantity or period.

4. Inspect the quote PDF.

The sample quote totals EUR 160: EUR 100 for chairs and EUR 60 for tables. Compare the customer, rental period, quantities and unit prices with the reservation before sending your own document.

5. Practise check-out and return.

Check out the confirmed reservation. On return, account for every unit. For a test, record one chair as damaged and verify that usable availability changes. Resolve the issue when that item becomes rentable again.

6. Make a backup.

Create a verified backup package and store it in a location you can identify later. CSV export provides readable records; use the application's supported backup/restore workflow for recovery.

Before using it for a real event.

  • Check dates, quantities, customer details and currency.
  • Review damaged/missing units before promising availability.
  • Keep a current backup and inspect the final PDF.
  • Use a separate process for payments, invoices and accounting.
